01 / Think first

Where do results come from?

02 / Learn

From discovery to results

  1. Crawling: automated programs follow links and discover pages that are available to them.
  2. Indexing: information about discovered pages is organised so it can be searched later.
  3. Ranking: a system orders matching indexed pages using signals it has chosen.

A search engine does not necessarily know every page. Pages can be unavailable, blocked, or not yet indexed. The order of results is selected by a ranking system; it is not a guarantee that the first result is the best answer for every person.

03 / Investigate

Search a tiny index

This offline model searches a small, already indexed collection. It matches all the words you enter and gives extra weight to words in a page title. Real systems use much more information and more complex ranking methods.

Try plants in space, then space plants. The result order can change because the model scores title matches.

  1. A guide to experiments with seeds and growing food on a spacecraft.
  2. Explore stars, planets, and constellations visible from Earth.
  3. Learn how leaves, roots, and flowers help plants grow.
  4. A mission checklist for equipment, crew, and supplies.

04 / Reflect

Check your learning

Reveal the ideas

Indexing organises information about pages. A page may not have been discovered or indexed, may be unavailable to a crawler, or may not match the query and ranking rules.
